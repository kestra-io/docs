## Start here

**Who this is for:** this guide is for the person who owns a vRA estate and has to decide what replaces it. You may have a renewal coming up, a mandate to move off VMware automation, or the need to scale workflows and the people who can work on them. Whatever is driving the change, you still face the same set of decisions.

The approach in this guide starts with three ideas:

<Cards columns={3}>
<Card kicker="01" title="Your environment is bigger than vRA, and most of it stays">

ServiceNow, Terraform, Ansible, your CMDB, IPAM, secrets manager and infrastructure targets usually stay where they are. vRA owns the workflow engine and parts of the catalog and deployment record around it. The migration is mostly about replacing that engine, reconnecting the systems around it, and deciding where the catalog and resource inventory should live afterwards.

</Card>
<Card kicker="02" title="What you move to matters more than how you move">

Replace vRA with another tool shaped like VMware and you may end up facing the same limitations again. The next orchestrator has to reach the environment that has grown around vRA, including cloud accounts, Kubernetes platforms and CI pipelines already doing infrastructure work.

</Card>
<Card kicker="03" title="This is a method, not a brochure">

We built this guide from experience working with [enterprise vRA estates](#section-2-5) ranging from a few hundred workflows to a few thousand. It covers the four stages of the migration, the tooling available for inventory and conversion, and a way to work out what the migration costs and when it pays back, using your own numbers.

</Card>
</Cards>

It also sets out why we think Kestra is the right destination, and walks through the four stages of the migration from assessing your estate to switching vRA off. Appendices [B](#appendix-b) and [C](#appendix-c) cover deployment, security and platform review.
